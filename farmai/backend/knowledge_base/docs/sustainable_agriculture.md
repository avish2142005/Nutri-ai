# Sustainable Agriculture Practices
## Integrated Pest Management (IPM)
Integrated Pest Management (IPM) is an effective and environmentally sensitive approach to pest management that relies on a combination of common-sense practices. IPM programs use current, comprehensive information on the life cycles of pests and their interaction with the environment. This information, in combination with available pest control methods, is used to manage pest damage by the most economical means, and with the least possible hazard to people, property, and the environment.

## Crop Rotation
Crop rotation is the practice of planting different crops sequentially on the same plot of land to improve soil health, optimize nutrients in the soil, and combat pest and weed pressure. For example, say a farmer has planted a field of corn. When the corn harvest is finished, he might plant beans, since corn consumes a lot of nitrogen and beans return nitrogen to the soil.

## Organic Practices
Organic agriculture is a production system that sustains the health of soils, ecosystems, and people. It relies on ecological processes, biodiversity and cycles adapted to local conditions, rather than the use of inputs with adverse effects.

## Irrigation Scheduling
Proper irrigation scheduling is critical for water conservation and crop health. Over-watering can lead to root rot and fungal diseases like late blight or powdery mildew. Under-watering stresses the plants, making them more susceptible to pests and reducing yield. Using soil moisture sensors and monitoring weather forecasts (rain probability) can help determine the optimal time to irrigate. 

## Soil Health and Nutrient Management
Healthy soil is the foundation of sustainable agriculture. Adding organic matter, like compost or well-rotted manure, improves soil structure, water retention, and microbial activity. Nitrogen, Phosphorus, and Potassium (NPK) are the primary macronutrients needed by plants. However, excessive use of synthetic fertilizers can lead to nutrient runoff and environmental pollution.

## Disease Prevention
Preventing diseases is often more effective than treating them. Key practices include:
1. Planting disease-resistant varieties.
2. Ensuring adequate spacing between plants to improve air circulation and reduce humidity.
3. Watering at the base of the plant (drip irrigation) rather than overhead to keep leaves dry.
4. Promptly removing and destroying infected plant debris.
