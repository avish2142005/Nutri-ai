# IPM and Crop Disease Knowledge Base

## Tomato Early Blight (Alternaria solani)
**Pathogen:** Alternaria solani (fungus)
**Conditions:** Favoured by warm temperatures (24–29°C), high humidity (>80%), and prolonged leaf wetness.

### Identification
- Dark brown/black lesions with concentric rings (target-board pattern) on older lower leaves
- Yellow halo around lesions
- Lesions coalesce; severe defoliation follows

### IPM Recommendations (Priority Order)
1. **Cultural (first line):** Remove and destroy infected leaf debris. Mulch base to reduce splash dispersal. Increase plant spacing for air flow. Avoid overhead irrigation; use drip.
2. **Biological:** Apply Trichoderma viride or Bacillus subtilis-based bio-fungicides. Spray 5% Neem Seed Kernel Extract (NSKE).
3. **Monitoring:** Scout weekly. Track humidity; spray before symptom spread if risk is high.
4. **Chemical Advisory (last resort):** Copper oxychloride, Mancozeb, or Azoxystrobin-based fungicides may be considered. Confirm with your local agriculture officer / Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK) before applying any chemical treatment.

## Tomato Late Blight (Phytophthora infestans)
**Pathogen:** Phytophthora infestans (oomycete)
**Conditions:** Cool temperatures (10–20°C), high humidity (>90%), cool nights.

### Identification
- Water-soaked, greasy-looking lesions on leaves, stems, and fruits
- White sporulation on undersides during humid mornings
- Spreads rapidly in cool, wet weather

### IPM Recommendations (Priority Order)
1. **Cultural:** Plant certified disease-free seed. Use raised beds for drainage. Destroy volunteer plants and infected debris promptly.
2. **Biological:** Copper-based bactericides/fungicides (copper hydroxide) have moderate efficacy and are OMRI-listed for organic use.
3. **Monitoring:** Use Blitecast or Smith Periods models to time sprays. Monitor weather forecasts for infection periods.
4. **Chemical Advisory:** Metalaxyl + Mancozeb, Cymoxanil combinations. Confirm with your local agriculture officer / Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK) before applying any chemical treatment.

## Tomato Leaf Mold (Fulvia fulva)
**Pathogen:** Fulvia fulva (fungus)
**Conditions:** High humidity (>85%), poor air circulation, greenhouse conditions.

### Identification
- Pale yellow spots on upper leaf surface
- Olive-green to brown velvety fungal growth on lower surface

### IPM Recommendations (Priority Order)
1. **Cultural:** Reduce humidity by improving ventilation. Reduce plant density.
2. **Biological:** Neem-based spray, Bacillus subtilis applications.
3. **Chemical Advisory:** Chlorothalonil, Mancozeb. Confirm with your local agriculture officer / Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK) before applying any chemical treatment.

## Tomato Yellow Leaf Curl Virus (TYLCV)
**Pathogen:** Tomato Yellow Leaf Curl Virus (Begomovirus) — transmitted by whitefly (Bemisia tabaci)
**Conditions:** Dry, hot weather promotes whitefly populations.

### Identification
- Upward curling and yellowing of young leaves
- Stunted plant growth
- Flowers drop; fruit set severely reduced

### IPM Recommendations (Priority Order)
1. **Cultural:** Use virus-resistant/tolerant varieties. Remove infected plants immediately. Use yellow sticky traps for monitoring.
2. **Biological:** Encourage natural enemies of whitefly (Encarsia, Eretmocerus parasitoids). Apply neem oil (2–3%) to deter feeding.
3. **Monitoring:** Count whitefly adults per leaf; apply threshold-based decisions (>5 adults/leaf = action required).
4. **Chemical Advisory (vector control):** Imidacloprid soil drench for whitefly if pressure is severe. Rotate modes of action. Confirm with your local agriculture officer / Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK) before applying any chemical treatment.

## Tomato Bacterial Spot (Xanthomonas spp.)
**Pathogen:** Xanthomonas vesicatoria (bacteria)
**Conditions:** Warm (24–30°C), wet weather; overhead irrigation promotes spread.

### Identification
- Small, dark, water-soaked spots on leaves, stems, and fruit
- Spots turn brown with yellow halos
- Fruit spots are raised, scab-like

### IPM Recommendations (Priority Order)
1. **Cultural:** Avoid overhead irrigation. Use certified pathogen-free seed. Practice 2–3 year crop rotation.
2. **Biological:** Copper hydroxide (OMRI-listed) applications. Bacillus subtilis-based products.
3. **Chemical Advisory:** Copper bactericides combined with mancozeb. Confirm with your local agriculture officer / Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK) before applying any chemical treatment.

## Powdery Mildew (general)
**Pathogen:** Various obligate fungi (Leveillula taurica on tomatoes; Erysiphe spp. on cucurbits)
**Conditions:** Warm days (20–30°C), cool nights; low humidity (unlike most fungi).

### Identification
- White powdery fungal coating on leaf surface
- Leaves curl and turn yellow

### IPM Recommendations (Priority Order)
1. **Cultural:** Use resistant varieties. Improve ventilation. Remove badly infected leaves.
2. **Biological:** Spray potassium bicarbonate (5g/L), sulfur-based organic fungicides, Neem oil.
3. **Chemical Advisory:** Triadimefon, Hexaconazole. Confirm with your local agriculture officer / Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK) before applying any chemical treatment.

## General IPM Principles
- **Scouting:** Regular (weekly) scouting is the foundation of IPM. Record pest and disease levels; act on Economic Threshold Levels (ETL), not prophylactically.
- **Cultural Controls:** Crop rotation, resistant varieties, sanitation, proper spacing, and avoiding overhead irrigation reduce disease pressure significantly.
- **Biological Controls:** Natural enemies, bio-fungicides (Trichoderma, Bacillus subtilis), and botanical extracts (neem) are the first choice after cultural methods.
- **Chemical Controls:** Only as last resort when threshold is exceeded. Always use least-toxic, targeted options. Rotate modes of action (FRAC/IRAC codes) to avoid resistance.
- **Safety First:** Confirm with your local agriculture officer / Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK) before applying any chemical treatment.
